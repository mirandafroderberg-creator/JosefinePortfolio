The Challenge

The challenge was to design a microfiber tea towel for Tao Teaware as part of a design competition.

The brief was to create something that captured the feeling of tea culture while still having a contemporary and playful expression. I wanted the design to feel connected to the history and traditions surrounding tea without making it feel old-fashioned.

My concept explored the idea of sharing tea, conversations and everyday moments, combining historical references with a more modern illustrative style.

The Goals

  • Create a tea towel inspired by tea culture and its history.
  • Combine traditional references with a contemporary expression.
  • Create an illustration that works as a physical textile product.
  • Explore storytelling through illustration and typography.
  • Create a design that feels playful, memorable and connected to Tao Teaware.

Research

I started by looking into tea culture and the visual history surrounding it. I explored Chinese porcelain, historical tea packaging and traditional motifs to understand the visual language associated with tea and how it has been represented over time.

I wanted to take inspiration from these references without simply recreating them. Instead, I looked for details, shapes and ideas that could be reinterpreted in a more contemporary way.

This research became the starting point for the illustration and helped me find a balance between something that felt connected to tea culture and something that felt like my own.

Concept Development

After exploring tea culture and its visual history, I started experimenting with different ideas for the design.

I wanted the illustration to feel like more than a decorative pattern. Tea is often connected to conversations, shared moments and the little accidents that happen along the way, which led me to the idea of “Teagether in pours and spills.”

I explored different motifs, compositions and colour combinations to find a visual style that felt playful while still connecting back to the traditional references from my research.

The final concept combines these references with a more contemporary illustration style, creating something that feels both familiar and unexpected.

Visual Design

Once I had settled on the concept, I focused on developing the illustration and making sure it worked as a tea towel design.

I experimented with colour, typography and composition to create a design that felt playful and cohesive. The illustration needed to work as a complete image while also remaining clear when printed on fabric.

I kept the visual style slightly imperfect and hand-drawn to give the design a more personal and playful feeling. The historical references from the research helped ground the illustration, while the typography and composition brought it into a more contemporary expression.
